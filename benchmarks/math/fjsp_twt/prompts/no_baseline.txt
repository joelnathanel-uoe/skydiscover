You are an expert in combinatorial optimization and scheduling.
Your task is to discover a high-performance construction heuristic
for the Flexible Job Shop Scheduling Problem with Total Weighted
Tardiness (FJSP-TWT).

Problem: Given n jobs (each a sequence of operations) and m machines,
assign each operation to one eligible machine and determine start times
to minimise total weighted tardiness: sum_i w_i * max(0, C_i - d_i),
where w_i is the weight, d_i the due date, and C_i the completion time
of job i.

Your heuristic must implement solve(instance) where instance is a dict:
  n_jobs (int): number of jobs
  n_machines (int): number of machines
  jobs (list of dicts): each with
    release_time (int), due_date (int), weight (float),
    operations (list of lists of [machine_id, proc_time] pairs)

Return a list of dicts, one per scheduled operation:
  {'job': int, 'op': int, 'machine': int, 'start': int, 'end': int}

Feasibility constraints that must be satisfied:
  1. Precedence: operations within a job must be scheduled in order —
     op k cannot start until op k-1 has finished.
  2. Release time: the first operation of job j cannot start before
     jobs[j]['release_time'].
  3. Machine capacity: each machine can process at most one operation
     at a time — no two operations on the same machine may overlap.
  4. Machine eligibility: each operation must be assigned to one of its
     listed eligible machines only.
  5. No preemption: once an operation starts it runs to completion.

Scoring: your heuristic is evaluated against a strong classical baseline.
Score = baseline_twt / (baseline_twt + your_twt) averaged over benchmark
instances — 0.5 ties the baseline, 1.0 means zero tardiness, below 0.5
means worse than the baseline. Maximise this. Invalid schedules score 0.

Restrictions:
  - Construction heuristic only — a greedy dispatching rule that builds
    the schedule step by step. No metaheuristics, search, or RL.
  - Python standard library + numpy only. No external solvers or packages
    that require installation (e.g. ortools, scipy).
